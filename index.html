Facilitator notes

  • Switch to “Low vision”, then “No colour”, and ask which failures got worse. Usually 1, 4 and 6.
  • Flip to “Fixed” and ask: which fix took the longest? Almost all of them are minutes of design work.

Name the person in the issue

A good ticket says who is stuck, what they’re trying to do, and what gets in their way. Pick a vague issue, or paste one of yours, and rewrite it.

Facilitator notes
  • The chips are a nudge, not a grade. They look for words like “contributor”, “trying to” and “can’t”.
  • Ask two pairs to read their rewrites aloud. Which one would you pick up first, and why?
  • Link back to the talk: people are easier to design for than tickets.

Facilitator notes
  • Have pairs predict which single toggle helps Lola most before trying it. Most groups pick plain language; many end up arguing for “Offer a human”.
  • Tie to the evidence: in the 2025 Stack Overflow survey, 46% of developers distrusted AI accuracy and only 33% trusted it. Showing sources and a way out is how interfaces earn trust.
  • Stretch: which toggle would you ship first if you only had one sprint?

Prompt for people

AI writes a lot of interface copy now. A lazy prompt gets a generic answer; a prompt that names the person gets one they can use. Pick a task, write your prompt, then run it next to the lazy one.

Facilitator notes
  • Pairs spend 5 minutes writing, 5 running and judging, 5 sharing. Ask one pair to read both answers aloud.
  • The last rubric question matters most: AI fills gaps with confident guesses. Good prompts tell it what it doesn’t know and ask it to say so.
  • Tie to the evidence: in the 2025 Stack Overflow survey, 66% of developers said their top AI frustration is answers that are “almost right, but not quite”.
  • Each run uses the viewer’s own Claude account and asks their permission the first time. Where AI isn’t available, the page shows written examples and says so.

Take it home

Ten minutes. Each person answers one question out loud, then writes one commitment on a sticky note.

Who did you forget?

Think of the last thing you shipped. Who would have struggled with it, and how would you have found out?

Where does AI help, and where does it stop?

Name one task you’ll hand to AI this month, and one decision you’ll keep for people.

What will you change on Monday?

One habit, one prompt template, one question in code review. Small and specific beats ambitious.
